# Reading 8 — Schmitter, Philippe C. (1970)

**Citation (WEB, accessed 2026-10-27):**
Schmitter, Philippe C. 1970. "A Revised Theory of Regional Integration." *International Organization* 24(4): 836-868. DOI: 10.1017/s0020818300017549.

**Status:** WEB — outside research, not from class slides. Do not present as professor content.

**1. What is "integration"?**
A process in which national units come to share part or all of their decisional authority with an emerging international organization. Schmitter formalizes integration theory by specifying the conditions, actors' strategies, and contradictions involved.

**2. What drives it?**
Schmitter identifies key contradictions: uncertainty, issue interdependence, and external socioeconomic dependence. Actors' strategies are influenced by perceived equity of benefits and crises. Integration proceeds through cyclical decision-making involving crises and subsequent strategy adjustments. External conditions are integrated into the model to enhance predictive capacity.

**3. What are the actors?**
National units (states) and the emerging international organization. Schmitter specifies seven potential strategies for national actors in response to integration dynamics.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Through a formalized model with testable hypotheses. Schmitter identifies seven actor strategies and specifies the conditions under which integration proceeds or stalls. The model is designed for empirical testing.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on Haas's neo-functionalism (Schmitter's "Three Neo-Functional Hypotheses," IO 23(1), 1969). Revises and formalizes Haas's theory: argues that integration is not automatic but involves crises, contradictions, and strategic choices. Also engages with Hansen's critique of neo-functionalism's limits in developing countries.

**Cluster:** Neo-functionalism / Institutions and governance.
