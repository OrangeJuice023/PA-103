# Reading 21 — Puchala, Donald J. (1970)

**Citation (WEB, accessed 2026-10-27):**
Puchala, Donald J. 1970. "International Transactions and Regional Integration." *International Organization* 24(4): 732-763. DOI: 10.1017/s0020818300017513.
- https://doi.org/10.1017/s0020818300017513
- https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/intorg/v24y1970i04p732-763_01.html

**Status:** WEB — outside research, not from class slides. Do not present as professor content.

**1. What is "integration"?**
A multidimensional phenomenon/process, much more complex than early integration theory imagined. Regional integration is not one thing; transaction analysis fits some aspects but not others.

**2. What drives it?**
International transactions (flows of economic exchange, communication, etc.) among units. Transactionalist logic: communication and exchange build community and integration.

**3. What are the actors?**
States/nations and transnational actors carrying on international transactions.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Via transaction analysis — operationalizing transaction flows as indicators of integration. The article is partly a stock-taking: early exponents overestimated transaction analysis; early critics over-dismissed it; a middle position recognizes partial utility.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on Deutsch et al. (*Political Community and the North Atlantic Area*) and Etzioni (transactional/community tradition). Argues against both the over-enthusiastic early transaction school and its dismissive critics; integration is multidimensional, so no single approach suffices.

**Cluster:** Transactionalism / Critique and measurement.
