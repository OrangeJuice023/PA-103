**1. What is "integration"?**
A process: international political integration occurs when nations come to regularly make and implement binding public decisions through collective institutions/processes rather than formally autonomous national means. Governments forego some attributes of sovereignty and decision-making autonomy. Expressed also as social community, security community, or economic union depending on the linkage (amity/confidence, peaceful expectations, economic exchange/factor mobility).

**2. What drives it?**
Linkages among nations: feelings of mutual amity, confidence, identification; leaders' expectations that common problems will be resolved without large-scale violence; concentrations of economic exchange and free circulation of productive factors. Integration is multidimensional (not one cause).

**3. What are the actors?**
Nations/governments, their leaders, populations, and collective institutions/processes. Governments create and commit common resources to common objectives.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Requires multivariate measurement across multiple analytical levels and dimensions (social community, security community, economic union). Later cited (e.g., UNU-CRIS) as identifying two dimensions of authority: delegation and pooling.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on Haas's neo-functionalism and Deutsch's transactional/community tradition. Argues against treating integration as unidimensional or measurable with a single indicator.

**Cluster:** Critique and measurement / Transactionalism.
