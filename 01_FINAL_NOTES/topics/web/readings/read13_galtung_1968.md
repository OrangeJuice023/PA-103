# Reading 13 — Galtung, Johan (1968)

**Citation (WEB, accessed 2026-10-27):**
Galtung, Johan. 1968. "A Structural Theory of Integration." *Journal of Peace Research* 5(4): 375-395. DOI: 10.1177/002234336800500404.

**Status:** WEB — outside research, not from class slides. Do not present as professor content.

**1. What is "integration"?**
A process whereby two or more actors form one new actor. Galtung insists on separating the definition from conditions and consequences: integration is the formation of a new actor, not the conditions that produce it or the outcomes that follow. Three types: territorial (merging territories), organizational (creating shared institutions), and associational (building social bonds/identity).

**2. What drives it?**
Galtung analyzes how each type satisfies basic human and social needs. Organizational and associational integration are complementary and always satisfy some needs. Territorial integration depends on communication and tends to decrease with increasing communication. Organizational integration leads to crises alleviated only by associational integration, and vice versa — a dialectical pattern.

**3. What are the actors?**
Nations, states, and social groups at various levels. Galtung applies the framework to both national and world integration.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Through structural analysis of how the three types of integration combine within and across nations. Galtung predicts a general world trend of decreasing territorial integration and a dialectical cycle from organizational to associational and back.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on sociological/structural theory (Etzioni, Merton). Argues against conflating definition, conditions, and consequences of integration. Argues against the assumption that integration is always territorial or that one type implies the others. The "basic problem of a substitute for decreasingly important territorial integration" is left unanswered — a deliberate open question.

**Cluster:** Critique and measurement.
