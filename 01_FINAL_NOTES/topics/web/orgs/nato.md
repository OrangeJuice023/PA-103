## 1. What it is
A political and military alliance of North American and European states based on collective defense: an armed attack against one member is considered an attack against all (Article 5 of the North Atlantic Treaty).

## 2. Founding
- **Date:** 4 April 1949.
- **Place:** Washington, D.C. (North Atlantic Treaty signed).
- **Founding members (12):** Belgium, Canada, Denmark, France, Iceland, Italy, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Norway, Portugal, the United Kingdom, the United States.
- Subsequent enlargements (Greece/Turkey 1952; West Germany 1955; Spain 1982; Central/Eastern European states from 1999; Finland 2023; Sweden 2024) → 32 members today.

## 3. Members
32 member states (including Finland and Sweden as of 2023–2024). Core: North America (US, Canada) + Western Europe, expanded to Central/Eastern Europe.

## 4. Purpose
Collective defense (Article 5) and the preservation of peace and security in the North Atlantic area; originally to deter Soviet aggression and bind the US to European security; after the Cold War, crisis management, cooperative security, and partnerships; Article 5 invoked once (after 9/11, 2001).

## 5. Relevance
The most durable collective-defense alliance; the European security architecture's backbone; relevance renewed by Russia's war on Ukraine (2022–present), enlargement debates, and burden-sharing (defense spending) tensions.

## 6. Theory lens
**Collective security / collective defense:** members pool sovereignty over defense and commit to mutual military response — the classic security-based cooperation model critiqued by Clark and Tarzi (readings 28–29), though NATO is narrower (a regional alliance, not universal collective security).
