## Five questions (North Star A3)

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| 1. What is integration? | How and why states cease to be wholly sovereign, voluntarily "mingling and merging" to gain new techniques for resolving conflict. A **process** leading to a political community; concerned with outcomes. [s.5] |
| 2. What drives it? | Depends on the pretheory: institution-building (federalism), transaction volume and trust (communications), spillover and self-interest (neo-functionalism). [s.16-19] |
| 3. Who are the actors? | States and the groups and elites that respond to tasks, transactions, perceptions and learning, rather than sovereignty and military power. [s.4] |
| 4. How is it measured? | No agreed measure: no shared dependent variable. Alternatives are scales and Lindberg and Scheingold's three outcomes; Haas proposes his own fix (below). [s.24-34] |
| 5. Builds on / against | Evaluates 15 years of research (1955-1970) and three approaches; draws on Lindberg and Scheingold. [s.6, 28] |

## I. Why study it [s.4-6]
- Rose from systematic US social science and political efforts to unify Europe after WWII.
- Aim: explain the **voluntary, noncoercive** creation of larger political units where states "self-consciously eschew the use of force".
- Shifts attention from sovereignty and military power to tasks, transactions, perceptions, learning.
- The main reason to study it is **normative**.
- Integration must be separated from regionalism, regional cooperation, organizations, movements, systems and subsystems.
- Theories are judged on three criteria: **describe** what happens, **explain** why stages occur, **predict** outcomes. At this stage none fully meets all three. [s.6]
- Prompt: what does "noncoercive only" rule out? [s.7]

## II. Empirical generalizations and their limits [s.9-13]
- **Empirical generalization:** a group of variables that explains an outcome in practice but is not part of a larger theory. "Floating". [s.10]
- **13 verified findings** grouped by: global, socialist blocs, Western Europe/EEC, late-developing (Africa, Latin America), external world. [s.11]
  - Common markets are the strongest driver of integration.
  - Perceived fairness decides whether interdependence feels good or bad.
  - In Africa integration is mostly symbolic.
  - Outside (exogenous) powers have unpredictable effects.
  - Overall a "mixed bag": different abstraction levels, unranked, unconnected.
- **Problem:** findings depend on assumptions made before research; they are only as strong as those assumptions. The end state is indefinite: where is integration supposed to end? [s.12]
- **Four gaps in knowledge** [s.13]
  1. Do variables that explain *starting* a union also explain *maintaining* it?
  2. Must national and regional decision-making styles be congruent?
  3. Is the developing world so different that it needs a different model?
  4. How do actors *learn*; do perceived benefits redefine interests?
- Prompt: is unranked data useful without a theory? [s.14]

## III. Three pretheories [s.16-19]
Not yet validated theories; they define variables and research strategies, assume different causal links, and use different methods.

| Approach | Core claim | Weakness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Federalist** | Political purposes and institutions transfer from national to regional level. Focus on institution-building, constitutional design, representation, division of powers. Split between ideological activists and theorist-observers. | Pure activism discredited in Europe and Africa; theorists moved toward observing "processes" like neo-functionalism. |
| **Communications** | Cybernetics applied to groups. Volume of transactions (trade, mail, tourists) measures community formation; transaction volume leads to trust leads to a **security community**. | Better at retroactive explanation than prediction; ignores message content; correlation, not causation. |
| **Neo-functional** | Borrows pluralist national-state behavior; self-interest and incremental decisions. **Spillover**: work on one problem creates unintended consequences and demands for integration in new sectors, leading to a political community. | Works for Europe, mostly predicts failure elsewhere. |

Each picks a finish line before collecting data. Prompt: why can't we just combine them? [s.20-21]

## IV. Toward a dependent variable [s.23-34]
- **Dependent variable:** the outcome a theory tries to explain or predict. [s.23]
- No agreed one, so findings are at inconsistent abstraction levels and **nonadditive**: they were never measuring the same thing. [s.24]
- **Three rival finish lines** [s.25]
  - Federalism: federal union (written constitution, divided powers, e.g. US, Switzerland).
  - Communications: security community (amalgamated or pluralistic; stopped expecting war).
  - Neo-functionalism: political community or union (central institutions, progressive centralization).
  - None is a true dependent variable; none can be observed or measured as occurring. [s.26]
- **Attempt 1, scales:** one endpoint becomes a point on, or convergence of, multidimensional scales. Problems: disagreement on which variables; assumes progress is always toward "higher" scores. A methodological aid, not an answer. [s.27]
- **Attempt 2, Lindberg and Scheingold's three outcomes** for any integrative task: **fulfillment** (task achieved), **retraction** (backslide/disintegration), **extension** (spreads into unanticipated areas). Originally for EEC/Euratom; Haas suggests wider use. Still needs a master concept to sum across issue areas. [s.28]
- **Haas's fix, layer 1: three heuristic terminal conditions** [s.30]
  - **Regional state:** one center, hierarchical.
  - **Regional commune:** no center, diffuse.
  - **Asymmetrical regional overlap:** multiple uneven centers.
- **Fix layer 2: authority-legitimacy transfer**, the measuring stick: has *authority* moved from national to regional institutions, and do citizens accept it as *legitimate*? [s.31]
- **Three-step evaluative ladder** (one sector, then all sectors, then the region) [s.32-33]
  1. In one sector, what mix of variables explains the trend (spillover, elite responsiveness, bargaining style)?
  2. Across all sectors, add up integrative and disintegrative trends using fulfillment, extension and retraction. A region does not move in one direction.
  3. For the whole region, apply authority-legitimacy transfer and ask which terminal condition it approaches.
- **EEC example:** agriculture shows high transfer, energy low; the uneven result matches asymmetrical overlap. [s.34]
- **Caveat:** terminal conditions have "no real-life counterparts"; the ladder still needs human judgment. Prompt: is an admittedly imaginary dependent variable useful, or a sophisticated admission of defeat? [s.34-35]

## V. Open questions and the pivot [s.36-37]
1. Do forces that start integration also sustain it?
2. Must national and regional politics move in sync? Haas: no; the EEC kept integrating economically while national politics became more nationalist.
3. Separate model for the developing world? Haas: same variables, different weights.
4. How does learning work? Haas's admitted biggest black box.

Haas proposes **computer simulation** (forces explicit, codeable assumptions). He closes by speculating whether a fragmented multilayered world or concentrated regional blocs is safer for peace. Prompt: does that normative leap follow from the analysis, or is it a value judgment where definitions run out? [s.37]
