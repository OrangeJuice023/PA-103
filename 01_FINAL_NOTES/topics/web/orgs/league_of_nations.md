## 1. What it is
The first permanent international organization with general (near-universal) membership created to maintain world peace; the predecessor of the United Nations; headquartered in Geneva.

## 2. Founding
- **Date:** founded 1919–1920 (Covenant adopted as part of the Treaty of Versailles, June 1919; League came into being 10 January 1920).
- **Place:** Geneva, Switzerland (headquarters).
- **Origin:** Woodrow Wilson's Fourteen Points; the Paris Peace Conference after World War I.
- **Dissolved:** 18 April 1946, with assets/functions transferred to the United Nations.

## 3. Members
At its peak ~58 members; notable non-members included the United States (Senate rejected the Treaty of Versailles/Covenant), and the USSR joined late (1934) and was expelled (1939); Germany, Japan, Italy withdrew in the 1930s.

## 4. Purpose
Collective security: prevent war through collective action, disarmament, negotiation/arbitration of disputes, and improving global welfare (health, labor, refugees, mandates system). The Covenant required members to respect one another's territory and to act against an aggressor.

## 5. Relevance
Failed to prevent the major acts of aggression of the 1930s (Manchuria 1931, Abyssinia 1935) and could not stop World War II — the classic case of collective security's weakness without great-power commitment and enforcement capacity; directly succeeded by the UN (1945), which kept collective security but added the Security Council and veto structure.

## 6. Theory lens
**Collective security (Clark, Tarzi — readings 28–29):** the paradigmatic general collective-security body; illustrates both the aspiration (peace through collective response to aggression) and the limits (absence of key powers, no enforcement teeth, sovereignty concerns) that Clark and Tarzi critique.
