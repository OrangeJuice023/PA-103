**1. What is "integration"?**
A process: neo-functionalist integration in which states assign supranational responsibility for a limited task, producing spillover into interdependent activities and wider regional authority, potentially ending in political integration. A revised model applied to common markets across regions.

**2. What drives it?**
Spillover (unintended external effects of delegated tasks), economic interdependence, and the common market as a first ("federalizing") step. Under democracy and pluralism, governments get entangled in regional pressures and concede wider authority.

**3. What are the actors?**
Member states, regional bureaucrats/secretariats, self-organized interests (interest associations, social movements) at the regional level, citizens whose expectations shift toward the region.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Comparative analysis of common markets across regions (concept and measurement); the revised model offers testable propositions rather than automatic predictions.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on Haas's neo-functionalism (and Schmitter's revised theory in the same IO 24(4) issue). Revises/corrects Haas: argues against teleological/automatic integration and against "political unification" as inevitable; integration is sporadic and conflictual.

**Cluster:** Neo-functionalism.
