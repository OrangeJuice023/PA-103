# Reading 9 — Hansen, Roger D. (1969)

**Citation (WEB, accessed 2026-10-27):**
Hansen, Roger D. 1969. "Regional Integration: Reflections on a Decade of Theoretical Efforts." *World Politics* 21(2): 242-271. DOI: 10.2307/2009822.

**Status:** WEB — outside research, not from class slides. Do not present as professor content.

**1. What is "integration"?**
Hansen reviews a decade of integration theory and finds a gap between theory and political reality. Integration theory explains or predicts regional integration almost exclusively on the basis of processes internal to the region, ignoring external factors (superpowers, global economy). Theory is also relatively silent about the role of superpowers.

**2. What drives it?**
Hansen argues that distribution crises must be expected in integration settings, especially in less developed countries, leading to "instant politicization." In Europe, such crises were avoided because of bargaining styles emphasizing package deals. For developing countries, neo-functionalism cannot be applied because economic welfare issues are "high politics," not technical matters.

**3. What are the actors?**
States, superpowers (external actors), and regional organizations. Hansen emphasizes that integration theory must account for external actors and power asymmetries.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Through comparative case analysis (Europe vs. Central America, East Africa). Hansen tests neo-functionalism's applicability across regions and finds it wanting for developing countries due to distribution crises and politicization.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on Haas's neo-functionalism and Deutsch's transactionalism. Argues against the assumption that neo-functionalism (developed for Europe) can be applied to developing countries. Argues that integration theory must incorporate external variables and distributional concerns. Critiques the "high politics vs. low politics" dichotomy.

**Cluster:** Critique and measurement.
