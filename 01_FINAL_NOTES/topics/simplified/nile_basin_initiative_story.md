# Nile Basin Initiative (NBI): The Story Version

*Full note: [nile_basin_initiative.md](../web/orgs/nile_basin_initiative.md). The story is an analogy for studying; the facts come from the NBI website and its corporate report (WEB, accessed 2026-10-27).*

## The one idea
**When countries share one river, the river itself can become the reason they learn to cooperate, even if they fight over everything else.**

## The story: ten countries and one river

Picture ten countries that all drink from the same river. Egypt, Ethiopia, Sudan, Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, Burundi, DR Congo, and South Sudan. Plus Eritrea, watching from the sidelines.

For decades, some of these countries shared pieces of the river's data: rainfall records, water levels, that kind of thing. But there was no single table where all ten sat together. Then, after the Cold War, the world became friendlier to regional cooperation. In 1999, all ten riparian states met in Dar es Salaam, Tanzania, and launched the Nile Basin Initiative (NBI). It was the first time every Nile country sat in one room to talk about the whole river.

Before NBI, the only cooperation was TECCONILE (est. 1992), a smaller group. NBI replaced it as a transitional arrangement, meant to last only until a permanent legal agreement, the Cooperative Framework Agreement (CFA), was signed.

## The three jobs of the NBI

Think of NBI as a neutral meeting room with three functions:

1. **Facilitating basin cooperation** - the only all-inclusive, neutral platform where riparians can discuss joint use of the Nile with trust and confidence.
2. **Water resources management** - shared data, early warning systems, technical capacity.
3. **Water resources development** - preparing climate-resilient cooperative investments in energy, water, and food.

To do this, NBI runs three centers: the Secretariat (Nile-SEC, in Entebbe, Uganda), ENTRO (Eastern Nile, in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia), and NELSAP-CU (Nile Equatorial Lakes, in Kigali, Rwanda). This is subsidiarity: one secretariat plus two regional programs.

## The big test: the Cooperative Framework Agreement (CFA)

The CFA was the permanent legal framework NBI was supposed to produce. It took more than a decade to negotiate. It opened for signature in Entebbe in 2010. But Egypt and Sudan froze their participation and refused to sign. DR Congo abstained. Only after six states ratified or acceded (Burundi, Ethiopia, Rwanda, Tanzania, Uganda; South Sudan acceded) did the CFA enter into force on **13 October 2024**. That triggered the Nile River Basin Commission (NRBC) to succeed NBI, but only six of the ten members had transitioned as of the 2025 Corporate Report.

Article 14, on water security, was reserved and annexed for later resolution. The core fight: upstream countries (Ethiopia) want to build dams like the GERD; downstream countries (Egypt, Sudan) fear losing water.

## Why this matters for PA 103

NBI is the textbook case of **functionalism** (Mitrany): cooperation on a concrete technical task (shared water management) builds trust, confidence, and habits of cooperation that can spill over into broader political integration. It is a non-European, Africa-based organization that shows functional demand can drive institution-building even where market interdependence is weak.

But NBI also shows the limits: distributional conflict over water allocation can stall legal settlement for decades. Cooperation on technical tasks does not automatically resolve who gets how much.

## Remember this
1. NBI launched **22 February 1999** in Dar es Salaam, Tanzania.
2. **10 member states** plus Eritrea as observer.
3. It replaced **TECCONILE** (1992) as a transitional arrangement.
4. Three centers: Nile-SEC (Entebbe), ENTRO (Addis Ababa), NELSAP-CU (Kigali).
5. Three core functions: facilitate cooperation, manage water resources, develop water resources.
6. CFA entered into force **13 October 2024** after ratification by six states.
7. Egypt and Sudan froze participation in 2010; DR Congo abstained.
8. Article 14 (water security) remains unresolved.
9. Theory lens: **functionalism** (Mitrany).

## Don't confuse
- *NBI vs NRBC:* NBI is the transitional initiative (1999). The NRBC is the permanent body that the CFA creates. They are not the same, and the transition is still incomplete.
- *Transitional vs permanent:* NBI was always meant to be temporary. The CFA is the permanent framework. Do not call NBI itself a permanent organization.
- *Frozen participation vs withdrawal:* Egypt and Sudan froze participation in 2010. That is not the same as leaving. They are still listed as member states.
