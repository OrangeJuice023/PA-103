## 1. What it is
A regional integration movement of Caribbean states promoting economic integration and cooperation; headquartered in Georgetown, Guyana; successor to CARIFTA (Caribbean Free Trade Association, 1965–1972).

## 2. Founding
- **Date:** 1 August 1973 (Treaty of Chaguaramas signed 4 July 1973, in force 1 August 1973).
- **Place:** Chaguaramas, Trinidad and Tobago.
- **Founding members (4):** Barbados, Guyana, Jamaica, Trinidad and Tobago. (Expanded to include most CARIFTA members and others.)

## 3. Members
15 full members + 5 associate members (as of mid-2020s): Antigua and Barbuda, Bahamas, Barbados, Belize, Dominica, Grenada, Guyana, Haiti, Jamaica, Montserrat (assoc.), Saint Kitts and Nevis, Saint Lucia, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, Suriname, Trinidad and Tobago; plus associate members (Anguilla, Bermuda, British Virgin Islands, Cayman Islands, Turks and Caicos).

## 4. Purpose
Improve standards of living and work; full employment; accelerated economic development; expanded trade and economic relations; enhanced international competitiveness; coordinated foreign economic policies; functional cooperation (health, education, culture, security — e.g., CARICOM Implementation Agency for Crime and Security, CARICOM Development Fund).

## 5. Relevance
The longest-running Caribbean integration project; achieved a single market (CARICOM Single Market and Economy, CSME, partially implemented) and a common external tariff; challenged by small size, debt, vulnerability, and uneven implementation — but also a model of functional cooperation (CARICOM agencies, Caribbean Court of Justice 2005).

## 6. Theory lens
**Economic integration (Balassa stages):** FTA (CARIFTA) → customs union/single market (CSME) ambition → deeper union; combined with strong **functional cooperation** via dedicated agencies — a mixed stage-functionalist Caribbean model.
