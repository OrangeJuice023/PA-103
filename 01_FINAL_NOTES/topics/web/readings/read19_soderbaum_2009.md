**Status:** WEB — outside research, not from class slides. Do not present as professor content.

**1. What is "integration"?**
Both a process and an object of comparison. Regional integration/regionalism is the growth of regional institution-building since the mid-1980s (EU widening/deepening, ASEAN, ECOWAS, NAFTA, SADC, Mercosur). It is plural and multidimensional: formal and informal regional governance, state and non-state actors, regional networks across issue areas.

**2. What drives it?**
Globalization and the shifting nature of global politics. New regionalism involves almost all governments plus non-state actors; drivers are political-economic and governance-related rather than a single cause.

**3. What are the actors?**
States (almost all governments), non-state actors, regional organizations, regional networks. Multiplicity of formal and informal governance.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Not by one metric. The chapter is methodological: it addresses "the problem of comparison" — what to compare, how to compare, why compare. It calls for systematic comparative analysis rather than ad hoc case comparisons.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on new regionalism and comparative regionalism literature. Argues against Euro-centrism (treating the EU as unique) and against the fragmented state of the field with weak debate over comparison fundamentals.

**Cluster:** Crisis and new regionalism.
