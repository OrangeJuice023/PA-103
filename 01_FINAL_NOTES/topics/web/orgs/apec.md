## 1. What it is
A regional economic forum of Pacific Rim economies promoting free trade and economic cooperation; operates by consensus and non-binding commitments; notable for "open regionalism" (benefits extend to non-members) and the absence of a treaty or secretariat with enforcement power.

## 2. Founding
- **Date:** November 1989.
- **Place:** Canberra, Australia (first ministerial meeting, hosted by Australian PM Bob Hawke).
- **Founding members (12):** Australia, Brunei, Canada, Indonesia, Japan, Korea (Republic of), Malaysia, New Zealand, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, United States. (Chile, China/Hong Kong, Mexico, Papua New Guinea, Peru, Russia, Taiwan/Chinese Taipei, Vietnam joined later → 21 members.)

## 3. Members
21 "member economies": Australia, Brunei, Canada, Chile, China, Hong Kong, China, Indonesia, Japan, Malaysia, Mexico, New Zealand, Papua New Guinea, Peru, Philippines, Republic of Korea, Russia, Singapore, Chinese Taipei, Thailand, United States, Vietnam.

## 4. Purpose
Support sustainable economic growth and prosperity in the Asia-Pacific; promote free and open trade and investment; reduce trade and investment barriers; foster economic and technical cooperation (ecotek); goals: Bogor Goals (free and open trade by 2010 for developed members, 2020 for developing members — not fully achieved).

## 5. Relevance
The leading Asia-Pacific economic forum; includes both the US and China; influential in shaping supply-chain and trade norms despite being non-binding; has been overshadowed somewhat by mega-regional deals (CPTPP, RCEP) and US–China rivalry.

## 6. Theory lens
**Open regionalism:** liberalizes trade among members while extending benefits non-discriminatorily (MFN-consistent), contrasting with preferential regional blocs; consensus-based, non-binding, and sovereignty-preserving — a loose forum rather than deep integration.
