# Reading 7 — Warleigh-Lack, Alex (2006)

**Citation (WEB, accessed 2026-10-27):**
Warleigh-Lack, Alex. 2006. "Towards a Conceptual Framework for Regionalisation: Bridging 'New Regionalism' and 'Integration Theory'." *Review of International Political Economy* 13(5): 750-771. DOI: 10.1080/09692290600950639.

**Status:** WEB — outside research, not from class slides. Do not present as professor content.

**1. What is "integration"?**
Warleigh-Lack distinguishes "regionalisation" (the process of cooperation, integration, and cohesion creating a regional space) from "regionalism" (the policy/project of region-building). He argues the divide between "new" and "old" regionalism is exaggerated and that both are sub-divisions of the same paradigm.

**2. What drives it?**
The paper argues that building a separate "New Regionalist Approach" is mistaken and falsely sets up classical integration theory as an "Other." Instead, Warleigh-Lack proposes a common conceptual framework with agreed independent variables, research issues, and hypotheses, all stemming from a common definition of the dependent variable ("regionalisation").

**3. What are the actors?**
Scholars in both EU studies and new regionalism, plus states and regional organizations. The paper is meta-theoretical, aimed at bridging sub-fields.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Through a typology of "regionalisation" as the dependent variable, plus four tentative hypotheses and a set of independent variables for empirical comparative research. Warleigh-Lack argues for reflexive, rigorous comparative methodology.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on both classical integration theory (Haas, Deutsch, Mitrany) and new regionalism literature (Hettne, Söderbaum). Argues against the "new regionalism" school's attempt to create a separate paradigm distinct from classical integration theory. Also argues against EU scholars who accept the divide by default, hampering useful theory.

**Cluster:** Crisis and new regionalism / Institutions and governance.
