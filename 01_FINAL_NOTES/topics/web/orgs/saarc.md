## 1. What it is
A regional intergovernmental organization of South Asian states promoting economic and social development and regional cooperation; headquartered in Kathmandu, Nepal; decisions by consensus; often described as one of the world's least integrated regional blocs.

## 2. Founding
- **Date:** 8 December 1985.
- **Place:** Dhaka, Bangladesh (first summit; charter adopted).
- **Founding members (7):** Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan, Sri Lanka. Afghanistan joined 2007 → 8 members.

## 3. Members
8 members: Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan, Sri Lanka.

## 4. Purpose
Promote the welfare and improve quality of life of South Asian peoples; accelerate economic growth, social progress, and cultural development; promote collective self-reliance; strengthen cooperation in economic, social, technical, scientific fields; cooperate with other developing countries and international organizations.

## 5. Relevance
Largely stalled in recent years: summits infrequent (the 2016 Islamabad summit was cancelled after the Uri attack and India's boycott), SAARC motor-vehicles/energy agreements limited; India–Pakistan rivalry is the central constraint, with South Asian states turning to sub-regional (BBIN) or bilateral arrangements instead.

## 6. Theory lens
**Why integration stalls:** a clear case where political rivalry (India–Pakistan), consensus rule (one member can block), and security concerns outweigh economic complementarity — illustrating limits of integration absent political accommodation; a counter-example to neo-functionalist spillover.
