# Reading 28 — Clark, Mark T. (1995)

**Citation (WEB, accessed 2026-10-27):**
Clark, Mark T. 1995. "The Trouble with Collective Security." *Orbis* 39(2): 237-258. DOI: 10.1016/0030-4387(95)90025-x. (Spring 1995; also republished by the Foreign Policy Research Institute, April 1, 1995.)
- https://doi.org/10.1016/0030-4387(95)90025-x
- https://www.fpri.org/article/1995/04/trouble-collective-security/

**Status:** WEB — outside research, not from class slides. Do not present as professor content.

**1. What is "integration"?**
Collective security as both an idea and an instrument of national-security policy — states agreeing that an attack on one is an attack on all, enforced collectively (not economic/political integration per se, but security-based cooperation among states).

**2. What drives it?**
Post-Cold War conditions: collapse of the bipolar balance of power, the U.S.-led Gulf War coalition, and the Clinton administration's preference for multilateral security through the UN; renewed attention to UN peacekeeping.

**3. What are the actors?**
The United Nations, the United States, coalition states, member states contributing to peacekeeping/enforcement.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Theoretical and practical problem-structure analysis; illustrative cases (Gulf War, UN peacekeeping) showing where collective-security theory fails in practice.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on collective-security theory and its post-Cold War revival. Argues against naive/unqualified collective security: problems in the theory have significant policy effects, and collective security is not a reliable instrument without addressing its theoretical weaknesses. Pairs with Tarzi's critique.

**Cluster:** Collective security.
