## 1. What it is
A short-lived 1963 regional association of Malaya (later Malaysia), the Philippines, and Indonesia — "Maphilindo" (Malaya–Philippines–Indonesia) — intended as a Malay regional bloc based on shared ethnolinguistic/cultural identity, soon overtaken by confrontation over the formation of Malaysia.

## 2. Founding
- **Date:** 1963 (conference in Bangkok).
- **Place:** Bangkok, Thailand (31 July – 3 August 1963).
- **Members (3):** Malaya, the Philippines, Indonesia.

## 3. Members
3 members: Malaya (federated into Malaysia in Sept 1963), the Philippines, Indonesia.

## 4. Purpose
Promote cooperation among the three Malay-majority countries on cultural, economic, and political grounds; resolve disputes (notably the Philippine claim to North Borneo/Sabah and Indonesian opposition to Malaysia — Konfrontasi); create a Malay regional bloc as an alternative to British-backed Malaysia.

## 5. Relevance
Short-lived (effectively collapsed by late 1963–1964 amid Indonesia–Malaysia confrontation and the Sabah dispute); relevant to Philippine history (Diosdado Macapagal's Maphilindo initiative, Sabah claim); a precursor-context for ASEAN (1967) and a case of identity-based regionalism derailed by inter-state territorial disputes.

## 6. Theory lens
**Identity-based regionalism:** integration premised on shared Malay identity and culture rather than institutions or markets; collapsed when territorial/security disputes (Sabah, Konfrontasi) overwhelmed cultural affinity — identity alone is insufficient without conflict management.
