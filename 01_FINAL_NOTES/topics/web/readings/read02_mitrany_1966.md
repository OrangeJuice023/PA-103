# Reading 2 — Mitrany, David (1966)

**Citation (WEB, accessed 2026-10-27):**
Mitrany, David. 1966. *A Working Peace System: An Argument for the Functional Development of International Organization.* Chicago: Quadrangle Press (orig. pamphlet 1943). DOI: 10.1007/978-1-349-14817-2_13.

**Status:** WEB — outside research, not from class slides. Do not present as professor content.

**1. What is "integration"?**
A process and method: the functional development of international organization. Integration is not a constitution or federal union but a web of international agencies that manage common needs across borders, gradually making borders less meaningful. "A working peace" rather than a protected peace.

**2. What drives it?**
Shared needs and common interests in specific sectors (transport, health, economic development, etc.). Functional agencies form around concrete tasks; as they grow, they weave an ever-spreading web of international relationships that transforms sovereignty without requiring political agreement on end-states.

**3. What are the actors?**
States, international functional agencies, and the technical experts who run them. Mitrany distrusts political/constitutional approaches; the functional approach bypasses governments' political divisions.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Not by a single metric. Mitrany predicts growth in functional international organizations (e.g., UN agencies, IBRD, IDA, UNESCO). Later scholars (Sewell 1966, Deutsch et al. 1957) tested aspects: functional communities are easier to achieve and more durable than federations, though the link to peace is not automatic.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Argues against federalism and constitutional approaches (which merely create larger political units and "renationalize" nationalism). Builds on liberal internationalism. Contrasts with neo-functionalism (Haas): Mitrany rejects teleological end-states; functionalism has no political goal, only task-based cooperation. Haas later built on Mitrany's functionalism but added spillover and political integration as goals.

**Cluster:** Functionalism.
