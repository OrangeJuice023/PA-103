## 1. What it is
A Eurasian political, economic, and security organization; headquartered in Beijing; grew out of the "Shanghai Five" and is often characterized as security-led regionalism involving China, Russia, and Central/South Asian states.

## 2. Founding
- **Precursor:** Shanghai Five, 26 April 1996 (China, Russia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan) — confidence-building in the border region / military force reduction.
- **SCO established:** 15 June 2001 (Shanghai), with the signing of the SCO Charter; Uzbekistan joined as a founding SCO member.
- **Charter:** in force 19 September 2003.

## 3. Members
10 members (after 2017/2023 expansions): China, Russia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, India (2017), Pakistan (2017), Iran (2023), Belarus (2023). Plus observer states and dialogue partners.

## 4. Purpose
Strengthen mutual trust and neighborliness; cooperate against terrorism, separatism, and extremism ("three evils"); promote political, economic, and cultural cooperation; maintain regional peace and security.

## 5. Relevance
The leading security-led regional bloc in Eurasia excluding the US; a counterweight to Western institutions in members' rhetoric; expanded significantly (India, Pakistan, Iran, Belarus), though internal diversity (India–Pakistan, China–Russia limits) constrains deep integration.

## 6. Theory lens
**Security-led regionalism:** integration driven first by security concerns (border stability, counter-terrorism, great-power management), with economic cooperation secondary — the reverse of neo-functionalist market-first logic; shows integration can be organized by great powers around security rather than by spillover.
