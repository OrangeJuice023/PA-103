# Reading 3 — Etzioni, Amitai (1963)

**Citation (WEB, accessed 2026-10-27):**
Etzioni, Amitai. 1963. "The Epigenesis of Political Communities at the International Level." *American Journal of Sociology* 68(4): 407-421. DOI: 10.1086/223398.

**Status:** WEB — outside research, not from class slides. Do not present as professor content.

**1. What is "integration"?**
A process of political community-building: "supranational unification" in which states move from separate political communities toward a higher-level union. Etzioni distinguishes "halfway integration" (like the EU, where states keep sovereignty in some areas but delegate in others) from "full integration" (complete political unification).

**2. What drives it?**
Shared values, social cohesion, and communitarian identity. Integration is not just economic spillover or institutional bargaining; it requires emotional bonds, shared norms, and a transnational identity. Etzioni's model is "epigenetic": units acquire new functions, starting in one sector, adding others in a functional sequence, with growth in performance, power, and communication capabilities.

**3. What are the actors?**
States, populations, leaders, and communities. Etzioni emphasizes the normative/moral dimension: legitimate authority, inclusive governance, fair representation, and mechanisms for conflict resolution. Balance between central authority and local autonomy is essential.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Through a functional-analysis model of social change (supplementing Parsons-Bales-Smelser differentiation). The model specifies the sector where integration starts, the functional sequence of adding sectors, and the relationship between performance, power, and communication growth.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on Deutsch (community/communication) and Haas (neo-functionalism). Argues that economic integration alone is insufficient without shared identity and values. Contrasts with Haas's market-driven spillover: Etzioni places identity and communitarian bonds at the core. Also contrasts with Riker's bargaining view and Deutsch's security-community emphasis.

**Cluster:** Transactionalism / Community.
