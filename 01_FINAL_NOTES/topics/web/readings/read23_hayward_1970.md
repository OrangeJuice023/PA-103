**1. What is "integration"?**
A process amenable to a single common conceptualization across both national and international levels — political integration as nations/political units progressively take on collective attributes. National integration and regional/international integration are similar phenomena, not fundamentally different.

**2. What drives it?**
Similar community-building processes at the national and international levels; the author (a national-integration scholar) argues the two literatures have much in common and can inform each other.

**3. What are the actors?**
Nations or other political units, populations, leaders, and communities at both national and international levels.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Through a common conceptual framework applied to both national and international cases; Hayward's related work on correlates of national political integration supplies the measurement logic.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on national integration theory (Deutsch, Etzioni, Almond, national political development literature). Argues against treating national and international integration studies as separate fields with little cross-reference; calls for cross-fertilization.

**Cluster:** Critique and measurement.
