# Reading 6 — Rosamond, Ben (2008)

**Citation (WEB, accessed 2026-10-27):**
Rosamond, Ben. 2008. "Rethinking Classical Integration Theory." In *Regionalisation and Global Governance: The Taming of Globalisation?*, eds. Andrew F. Cooper, Christopher W. Hughes, Philippe De Lombaerde, 80-96. London: Routledge.

**Status:** WEB — outside research, not from class slides. Do not present as professor content.

**1. What is "integration"?**
Classical integration theory (neo-functionalism, transactionalism, federalism) studied European integration as a unique process. Rosamond rethinks this by situating it within broader global governance and regionalization debates, questioning the EU-centric assumptions of classical theory.

**2. What drives it?**
The chapter reflects on the relationship between regionalization and global governance, and how classical integration theory's tools (developed for the EU) may or may not travel to other regions. It surveys the theoretical debates around regionalism beyond Europe.

**3. What are the actors?**
Scholars, policymakers, and regional organizations. The chapter is a meta-theoretical reflection rather than a single-driver account.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Through critical engagement with the classical theoretical canon (Haas, Deutsch, Mitrany, Etzioni) and its applicability to contemporary regionalism. Rosamond examines the relationship between EU studies and international studies.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on classical integration theory (Haas neo-functionalism, Deutsch transactionalism, Mitrany functionalism). Argues against treating the EU as the universal model of integration and against the assumption that classical theories developed for Europe automatically apply to other regions. Calls for rethinking the relationship between EU studies and the study of regionalism elsewhere.

**Cluster:** Crisis and new regionalism.
