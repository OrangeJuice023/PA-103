# Reading 17 — Nye, Joseph S. (1968)

**Citation (WEB, accessed 2026-10-27):**
Nye, Joseph S. 1968. "Comparative Regional Integration: Concept and Measurement." *International Organization* 22(4): 855-880. DOI: 10.1017/s0020818300013837.

**Status:** WEB — outside research, not from class slides. Do not present as professor content.

**1. What is "integration"?**
Nye defines integration as "a process leading to political community — a condition in which a group of people recognizes mutual obligations and some notion of a common interest." He argues that differences in conceptualization and measurement of the dependent variable (integration) have led theorists to "talk past each other."

**2. What drives it?**
Nye's focus is methodological: how to define and measure integration so that comparative analysis becomes possible. He identifies problems in the field: (1) difficulty relating different authors' concepts to each other; (2) disputes made harder to resolve by conceptualization differences. He proposes a framework for comparative measurement.

**3. What are the actors?**
States and regional organizations. Nye's revised neo-functionalist model (1970) adds interest groups, regional bureaucrats, and citizens.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Through comparative analysis across regions. Nye proposes moving from single-case European studies to systematic comparison. His later work (1970, "Comparing Common Markets") revises neo-functionalism with testable propositions rather than automatic predictions.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on Haas's neo-functionalism. Argues against the assumption that economic integration automatically produces political unity — integration is conditional, not automatic. Argues against Euro-centrism and for comparative regional integration across regions. His work is foundational for the comparative regionalism field.

**Cluster:** Critique and measurement / Neo-functionalism.
