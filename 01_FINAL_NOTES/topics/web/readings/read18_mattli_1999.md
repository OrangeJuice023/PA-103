# Reading 18 — Mattli, Walter (1999)

**Citation (WEB, accessed 2026-10-27):**
Mattli, Walter. 1999. "Explaining Regional Integration Outcomes." *Journal of European Public Policy* 6(1): 1-27. DOI: 10.1080/135017699343775.

**Status:** WEB — outside research, not from class slides. Do not present as professor content.

**1. What is "integration"?**
A process with widely varying outcomes: from highly successful (EU) to complete failure (LAFTA, Andean Pact, ECOWAS). Mattli seeks to explain this variation rather than assuming integration follows a single path.

**2. What drives it?**
Two sets of conditions must be met for integration to succeed: (1) demand-side conditions — the benefits of integration must exceed the costs for member states; (2) supply-side conditions — institutional capacity and leadership to manage integration. Regional groups satisfying both conditions stand the greatest chance of succeeding; those fulfilling neither are least likely to attain significant integration.

**3. What are the actors?**
Member states, regional organizations, and institutional leaders. Mattli focuses on the political economy of integration: why some groups succeed and others fail.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Through comparative analysis of various integration schemes (EU, LAFTA, Andean Pact, ECOWAS, etc.). Mattli tests the demand-supply framework against historical cases to explain variation in outcomes.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on institutional theories and the political economy of integration. Argues against single-factor explanations (market spillover alone, or institutional design alone). Argues that both demand (economic benefits) and supply (institutional capacity) are necessary. Also relates to his later work on Ernst Haas's evolving thinking on comparative regional integration.

**Cluster:** Institutions and governance.
