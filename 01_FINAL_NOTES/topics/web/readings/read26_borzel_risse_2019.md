**1. What is "integration"?**
A process: the pooling and delegating of authority in regional institutions; multidimensional, understood comparatively across world regions rather than as a European-only endpoint.

**2. What drives it?**
Functional demand: security interdependence and the quest for regime stability (democratic or authoritarian) — more important than intra-regional economic interdependence outside Europe. Supply side: elite regional-identity construction and community-building that resonates with mass public opinion. Plus diffusion of institutional designs (EU emulation across regions).

**3. What are the actors?**
Political and other elites, states, regional institutions, citizens/mass public opinion (whose identity resonance generates support), and diffusing models (notably the EU).

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Mapping intra-regional trade (proxy for economic interdependence) against the density/strength of regionalism across major world regions; finding little correlation outside Europe; comparative regionalism across the globe.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on post-functionalism (Hooghe and Marks). Argues against neo-functionalism and liberal intergovernmentalism for their European/liberal bias and for privileging economic interdependence; "decenters" Europe; claims standard theories travel poorly beyond Europe.

**Cluster:** Crisis and new regionalism / Institutions and governance.
