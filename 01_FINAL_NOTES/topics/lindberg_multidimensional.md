## Central argument

Political integration is the **evolution of a collective decision-making system among nations**. Its properties are interrelated but can change at different rates and in different directions, so researchers need several measures to *describe* the changing system before *explaining* it. Adding ten scores with equal weights hides differences unless a theory justifies the combination. [p.1]

His contribution is methodological: making political integration observable and comparable. [p.1]

## Five questions (North Star A3)

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| 1. What is integration? | A process over time: the evolution of a collective decision-making system among nations (art. p.650). The evidence is regularized joint involvement in authoritative public decisions. [p.2] |
| 2. What drives it? | Activators (demand flow, resources, leadership, bargaining) interacting with consequences that feed back into the system (spillover, spill-back, encapsulation). [p.3, 7] |
| 3. Who are the actors? | Governments, officials, groups and citizens who make demands, lead, bargain, comply or bear costs. [p.5-6] |
| 4. How is it measured? | A **profile of ten properties** compared across time and issue areas, with indicators and coding rules. Not one score. [p.3, 7, 14] |
| 5. Builds on / against | Haas (dependent-variable debate; Lindberg also warns against premature aggregation), Nye (separates meanings and measures), Deutsch (transactions and security community, which do not measure collective authority). [p.14] |

## What integration is not (concept comparison) [p.2]

| Concept | Describes | Why it is not political integration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social community | Trust, attachment, identification | Shared identity does not establish joint public decisions |
| Security community | Reliable expectations of peaceful dispute settlement | Peace can coexist with autonomous national decisions |
| Economic union | Exchange and factor movement | Trade volume is not collective authority |
| Political integration | An evolving collective decision-making system | The thing Lindberg measures |

Cooperation can stay occasional and nationally autonomous; political integration needs a regular collective process with consequential authority. [p.2]

## The ten properties (3-4-3) [p.3]

The 3-4-3 grouping is a **memory aid**, not Lindberg's name for a framework. Source: article pp. 657-659.

| Group | # | Property | Question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Level | 1 | Functional scope | Which policy areas, and how important? |
| Level | 2 | Range of decision stages | How far through the decision process? |
| Level | 3 | Relative decisiveness | Weight of collective vs national choices? |
| Activators | 4 | Demand flow | Which claims go to the collective arena, from whom, how strong? |
| Activators | 5 | Resources | What institutional, legal, financial, political capacity exists? |
| Activators | 6 | Leadership | Who mobilizes capacity and advances action? |
| Activators | 7 | Bargaining modalities | How do actors negotiate competing interests? |
| Consequences | 8 | Penetrativeness | Whose behavior changes, by how much? |
| Consequences | 9 | Compliance | Are decisions accepted, implemented, observed? |
| Consequences | 10 | Distributive consequences | Who gets benefits and bears costs? |

**Why a profile:** a system can cover many areas while nations keep decisive control, or cover few areas but decide the central ones, or have active institutions with weak compliance. One indicator cannot capture that (art. p.651). The properties are parts of one system, distinct but not independent. [p.3]

## Property notes

### Level [p.4]
- **Scope:** do not just count areas; they differ in salience (art. p.665).
- **Range of decision stages:** problem-recognition, decision, application (art. pp.668-671). Figure 4 has six ordinal ranks, from joint information gathering up to collective institutions administering and enforcing binding rules. National administration of binding collective rules does **not** automatically mean weak collective authority.
- **Relative decisiveness:** weight of the collective arena in the most important choices of an issue area. Lindberg's 1950s NATO example: few areas but central policy questions (art. p.672).

### Activators [p.5]
- **Demand flow:** "the system's lifeblood" (art. p.679). A serious problem is not itself demand; trace requests directed to the collective arena.
- **Resources:** agreements, institutions, legal authority, information, money, legitimacy, support, compliance experience. A treaty creates a capacity; funds, staff and effective authority need separate checking.
- **Leadership:** resources are "action potentials" that leaders must use (art. p.697). A signature records agreement, not leadership.
- **Bargaining modalities:** competitive (separate gains) vs variable-sum (joint gains via concessions, linked issues, common interest). Conflict inside accepted procedures can coexist with integration.

### Consequences [p.6]
- **Penetrativeness:** whose behavior changes (art. pp.709-711). A rule is not proof of behavior change; attribution matters (prices, weather, technology, national policy).
- **Compliance:** monitoring and enforcement records. A detected violation can mean a problem *or* active enforcement.
- **Distribution:** who gains, who pays. Unequal gains do not automatically mean more or less integration; it depends on how winners and losers respond in later rounds.

## Feedback over time [p.7]

Past decisions reshape later demands, resources, leadership, bargaining, compliance and level of authority (art. p.725).

| Direction | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Spillover | Past experience leads to more integration |
| Spill-back | Past experience leads to less integration |
| Encapsulation | System stabilizes at its level, containing pressures |

Disappointment is not automatically spill-back; success is not automatically spillover. A review that informs a later plan shows a *feedback process*, but its *direction* needs further evidence.

**Aggregation:** Lindberg proposes ten ranges of variation (art. p.719) and explores combined measures, but objects to *premature* aggregation; weights must be justified by theory (art. pp.724-725).

## The three cases in the guide

These are **applications of Lindberg's concepts**, not cases he analysed in 1970. [p.1] Spillover labels are hypotheses, not findings.

| | EU customs [p.8-9] | ASEAN nursing MRA [p.10-11] | ASEAN haze [p.12-13] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Issue area | Customs and trade in goods | Recognition and mobility of nurses | Haze from land and forest fires |
| Collective vs national | Common rules and tariff; national customs administration | Regional eligibility conditions; national assessment and licensing | Regional monitoring and coordination; national prevention and response |
| Key facts | Customs union began 1968; Commission proposal May 2023; Council-Parliament agreement March 2026; Council approval September 2026; 75% of customs duties went to EU budget in 2024 | MRA signed in Cebu, 8 December 2006; host-country laws and regulators keep licensing; a home licence alone is insufficient | Fires 1997-1998 then 2002 ASEAN Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution; second Haze-Free Roadmap adopted August 2023; 2024 release: about 3x hotspots in southern ASEAN in 2023 vs 2022, tied to El Nino |
| Caveats | The 75% is revenue allocation, not who bears tariff cost; counterfeit detentions show enforcement, not a compliance rate | Legal provisions do not show actual migration; wages, language, immigration rules also matter | Hotspot counts do not alone show the agreement's causal effect; a revised roadmap is not proof of deeper authority |

## Limits of the framework [p.14]
- Needs a lot of data and judgment about salience, behavior and causation.
- Operational suggestions are tentative; reliability and validity untested.
- Many illustrations draw on European Community experience, so other cases need adaptation, not automatic transfer.

## How to apply the method [p.14-15]
Define the political object and period, define each property, choose indicators and coding rules, collect legal provisions, decisions, negotiation histories, budgets and implementation records, compare profiles over time, test alternative causes, justify any weighting. Compare the **same property in a defined issue area and period**; the cases do not rank whole organizations.

## Review Q&A (condensed from the guide) [p.16]
- **Main argument?** Integration is an evolving collective decision-making system whose interrelated properties change unevenly, so many measures are needed.
- **Is 3-4-3 Lindberg's name?** No, a memory aid.
- **National implementation = weak integration?** Not necessarily.
- **Nursing recognition not automatic?** Host-country laws and regulators keep assessment and licensing.
- **Signing = leadership and demand flow?** No; that needs evidence of proposal-making, support-building, brokerage, and actual claims.
- **New haze roadmap = spillover?** No; it documents a response to experience, not an increase in collective decision-making.
- **Can ten properties be summed?** Only with justified weights; avoid premature aggregation.
