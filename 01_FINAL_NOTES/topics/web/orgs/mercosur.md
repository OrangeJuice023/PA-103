## 1. What it is
A South American customs union and common-market project among Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, and Uruguay (with Venezuela suspended); headquartered in Montevideo, Uruguay (permanent secretariat); the region's most ambitious integration scheme after the EU model.

## 2. Founding
- **Date:** Treaty of Asunción signed 26 March 1991 (in force 29 November 1991); Protocol of Ouro Preto (17 December 1994) gave it international legal personality and a permanent structure.
- **Place:** Asunción, Paraguay (Treaty of Asunción).
- **Founding members (4):** Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, Uruguay. (Venezuela joined 2012, suspended 2016.)

## 3. Members
4 full members: Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, Uruguay. Venezuela (suspended since 1 December 2016). Associated states: Bolivia (in process of full membership), Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, Guyana, Peru, Suriname (various association levels).

## 4. Purpose
Free movement of goods, services, and factors of production; common external tariff; coordinate macroeconomic and sectoral policies; common trade policy vis-à-vis third countries; ultimately a common market (customs union largely achieved, common market incomplete).

## 5. Relevance
South America's main integration bloc; illustrates both the promise (intra-regional trade growth, Brazil–Argentina anchor) and limits (economic crises, asymmetric members, Venezuela suspension, political polarization, slow common-market completion) of developing-region integration.

## 6. Theory lens
**Balassa stages:** explicitly designed as free-trade area → customs union → common market → (long-term) economic union — a Balassa-style stage sequence; shows stage-based integration can stall at the customs-union/common-market threshold when macroeconomic and political conditions diverge.
