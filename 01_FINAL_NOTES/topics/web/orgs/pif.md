## 1. What it is
An intergovernmental organization of Pacific island states and territories promoting cooperation among members; headquartered in Suva, Fiji; the principal regional body for the Pacific Islands, known for the Biketawa Declaration (2000) on security/governance and the 2050 Strategy for the Blue Pacific Continent.

## 2. Founding
- **Date:** 5 August 1971 (founded as the South Pacific Forum).
- **Place:** Wellington, New Zealand (first meeting of independent island states).
- **Renamed:** Pacific Islands Forum in 1999/2000.

## 3. Members
18 members (as of mid-2020s): Australia, Cook Islands, Federated States of Micronesia, Fiji, Kiribati, Marshall Islands, Nauru, New Zealand, Niue, Palau, Papua New Guinea, Samoa, Solomon Islands, Tonga, Tuvalu, Vanuatu, plus French Polynesia and New Caledonia (associated members, later full members from 2016). (Note: sub-regional groupings — the Micronesian sub-group briefly broke away 2021–2022 but returned.)

## 4. Purpose
Promote the economic and social well-being of Pacific peoples; protect and sustain ocean resources; regional security and governance (Biketawa Declaration allows collective response to crises); climate change advocacy (the Forum is a leading small-state voice on climate/sea-level rise); the 2050 Strategy for the Blue Pacific Continent (2022).

## 5. Relevance
A small-state regionalism punch above its weight on climate and ocean governance; shows microstates can build a cohesive regional voice; challenged by great-power competition (China–US) in the Pacific and internal sub-regional splits.

## 6. Theory lens
**Small-state regionalism:** integration among microstates driven by shared vulnerability (climate, oceans, remoteness) and the need for collective voice in global forums; limited by small capacities, dispersion, and aid dependence.
