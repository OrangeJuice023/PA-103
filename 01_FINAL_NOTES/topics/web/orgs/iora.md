## 1. What it is
An intergovernmental organization of Indian Ocean coastal states promoting regional cooperation and sustainable development; headquartered in Mauritius; based on the principle of open regionalism and consensus.

## 2. Founding
- **Date:** March 1997.
- **Place:** Mauritius (the Mauritius Declaration, launched by South Africa's Nelson Mandela).
- **Founding members (7):** Australia, India, Indonesia, Kenya, Malaysia, Singapore, South Africa, Tanzania, Thailand (and others at inception); has since expanded to ~23 members + 10 dialogue partners.

## 3. Members
23 member states around the Indian Ocean rim (Africa, Middle East, South Asia, Southeast Asia, Australia), e.g., Australia, Bangladesh, India, Indonesia, Iran, Kenya, Madagascar, Malaysia, Maldives, Mauritius, Mozambique, Oman, Seychelles, Singapore, Somalia, South Africa, Sri Lanka, Tanzania, Thailand, UAE, Yemen, plus others; dialogue partners include China, Egypt, France, Japan, UK, US.

## 4. Purpose
Promote sustainable growth and balanced development of the Indian Ocean region; focus on maritime safety/security, trade and investment facilitation, fisheries, tourism, disaster risk management, and the blue economy; IORA works through six priority areas.

## 5. Relevance
A broad ocean-rim forum (not a tightly integrated bloc); gains attention as Indo-Pacific competition rises and the Indian Ocean's trade/energy lanes grow more strategic; limited institutional depth and consensus rule keep it consultative rather than integrative.

## 6. Theory lens
**New regionalism:** a post-Cold War, extra-European regional forum driven by maritime geography and shared economic/ecological concerns rather than by security alliance or market spillover; open and flexible, reflecting new-regionalism's multiplicity of overlapping forums.
