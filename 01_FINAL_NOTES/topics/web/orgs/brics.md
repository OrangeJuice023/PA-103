## 1. What it is
A bloc of major emerging economies (originally Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa) that coordinates on global economic and political governance; expanded in 2024; not a formal regional integration organization with a treaty, secretariat, or common market.

## 2. Founding
- **Origin:** "BRIC" term coined 2001 (Goldman Sachs economist Jim O'Neill); first BRIC foreign ministers' meeting 2006; first BRIC summit 2009 (Yekaterinburg, Russia).
- **South Africa joined:** December 2010 → BRICS.
- **2024 expansion:** BRICS+ admitted Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE (effective 1 January 2025) at the 2023 Johannesburg summit decision; Kazan summit 2024 (Russia) consolidated the enlarged group and discussed "BRICS partner countries."

## 3. Members
5 originals: Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa. Post-2024 enlargement: plus Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, Saudi Arabia, UAE (10 members); plus a growing list of "partner countries."

## 4. Purpose
Reform global governance institutions (IMF, World Bank, UN Security Council); amplify Global South/emerging-market voices; establish alternatives such as the New Development Bank (NDB, 2014) and the Contingent Reserve Arrangement (CRA); coordinate positions on trade, development, climate, and security.

## 5. Relevance
The flagship "non-Western" bloc; illustrates the diffusion of power to emerging economies; expansion to BRICS+ signals an effort to build a broader Global South coalition; relevance in de-dollarization debates and Ukraine-war-era non-alignment.

## 6. Theory lens
**Beyond classical theory:** a bloc, not a formal integration project — no supranational institutions, no common market, no pooled sovereignty; fits neither neo-functionalism nor Balassa's stages, showing "regionalism"/bloc-building can be loose, issue-based, and global rather than regionally bounded.
