## 1. What it is
A Latin American free-trade association (1960–1980), formally the Asociación Latinoamericana de Libre Comercio (ALALC), replaced in 1980 by the Latin American Integration Association (ALADI/LAIA); an early attempt at regional free trade in the developing world.

## 2. Founding
- **Date:** Treaty of Montevideo signed 18 February 1960 (in force 2 June 1961).
- **Place:** Montevideo, Uruguay.
- **Founding members (7):** Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, Mexico, Peru, Uruguay (and Venezuela joined 1966 → 11 members).

## 3. Members
11 members at peak: Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, Mexico, Paraguay, Peru, Uruguay, Venezuela. (Paraguay joined 1967.)

## 4. Purpose
Progressive establishment of a free trade area in Latin America through automatic linear tariff reductions and reciprocity; promote regional industrial development and intra-regional trade; eventual common market aspiration.

## 5. Relevance
LAFTA failed to achieve deep liberalization: tariff reductions were slow, product coverage limited, and the "most favored nation" clause (benefits extended to all members regardless of reciprocity) weakened incentives; replaced by ALADI in 1980, which adopted a more flexible, partial-scope approach — a classic case of why early free-trade-area projects stalled.

## 6. Theory lens
**Free trade area stage / why it failed (Balassa):** LAFTA is the Balassa "free trade area" stage that stalled — shows that linear tariff cuts without reciprocity, with weak institutions and protectionist structures, produce little integration; a counter-example to both neo-functionalist spillover and successful market integration.
