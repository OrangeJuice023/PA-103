## 1. What it is
A regional intergovernmental organization of Southeast Asian states promoting political, economic, and socio-cultural cooperation among members, notable for consensus-based decision-making and the principle of non-interference in internal affairs (the "ASEAN Way").

## 2. Founding
- **Date:** 8 August 1967.
- **Place:** Bangkok, Thailand (Bangkok Declaration / ASEAN Declaration).
- **Founding members (5):** Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand.
- Brunei joined 1984; Vietnam 1995; Laos and Myanmar 1997; Cambodia 1999 → 10 members today.

## 3. Members
10 member states: Brunei, Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam. (The Philippines is a founding member — relevant given the course's Philippine context.)

## 4. Purpose
Accelerate economic growth, social progress, and cultural development; promote regional peace and stability; promote active collaboration and mutual assistance; provide mutual assistance in training/research; collaborate in agriculture, industry, trade, transport, etc.; maintain close cooperation with existing international/regional organizations.

## 5. Relevance
The leading example of "new regionalism" outside Europe: loose, intergovernmental, consensus-driven, non-supranational, respecting sovereignty — a deliberate contrast to the EU model; central to the Indo-Pacific and to ASEAN-led fora (ASEAN+3, East Asia Summit, ASEAN Regional Forum).

## 6. Theory lens
**New regionalism** / Southeast Asian contrast to the EU: integration through intergovernmental cooperation and informal norms rather than supranational delegation; community-building (ASEAN Community: Political-Security, Economic, Socio-Cultural pillars) echoing Etzioni/Deutsch on identity and community, but without spillover to supranational authority.
