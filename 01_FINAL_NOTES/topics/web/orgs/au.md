## 1. What it is
A continental union of 55 African states launched in 2002, succeeding the Organization of African Unity (OAU, 1963–2002); headquartered in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia; more interventionist than the OAU (e.g., peace and security architecture, possible intervention in grave circumstances).

## 2. Founding
- **Date:** launched 9 July 2002 (Durban, South Africa); Constitutive Act adopted 11 July 2000 (Lomé, Togo), in force 26 May 2001.
- **Predecessor:** Organization of African Unity (OAU), founded 25 May 1963 (Addis Ababa).
- **Key body:** Assembly of Heads of State and Government; AU Commission (secretariat); Peace and Security Council; Pan-African Parliament.

## 3. Members
55 member states (all recognized African states; Morocco rejoined 2017 after leaving the OAU in 1984 over Western Sahara).

## 4. Purpose
Promote unity and solidarity among African states; defend sovereignty and territorial integrity; accelerate political and socio-economic integration; promote peace, security, and stability; promote democratic principles/institutions and good governance; protect human rights; promote sustainable development (Agenda 2063).

## 5. Relevance
The continent's overarching regional organization; illustrates post-colonial regionalism focused first on decolonization/sovereignty (OAU) then on integration and peace/security (AU); challenged by coups, conflicts, financing dependence, and uneven commitment.

## 6. Theory lens
**Gibb on Africa** (reading 27): AU/African regionalism as state-led and elitist, constrained by neo-patrimonial sovereignty norms; contrasts with the European market-integration model. Also relates to functional cooperation (peace and security architecture) and comparative regionalism beyond Europe (Börzel & Risse, reading 26).
