I. Why Study Regional Integration?

===== PAGE 4 =====
Why Study Regional Integration?
Historical Context
The study emerged from the rise of systematic social science in the U.S. and
political efforts to unify Europe after WWII
Core Objective
To explain the voluntary, noncoercive creation of larger political  units where
states "self-consciously eschew the use of force" in their relations.
Shifts attention away from concepts such as sovereignty and military power
toward tasks, transactions, perceptions, and learning. 
The main reason for studying regional integration is normative.

===== PAGE 5 =====
Defining the Field
Integration must be distinguished from regionalism, regional cooperation,
regional organizations, movements, systems, and subsystems.   to avoid
confusion. 
Regional Integration is concerned with the study of how and why states cease
to be wholly sovereign, voluntarily "mingling and merging" to acquire new
techniques for resolving conflict.
Integration is concerned with outcomes
Integration is viewed as a process leading to the creation of a political
community.

===== PAGE 6 =====
Evaluating 15 Years of Research (1955-1970)
Existing theories are evaluated on three criteria: 
Their abilitity to describe what happens
Explain why stages occur
Predict future outcomes
Haas (1970) notes that at this stage, no theory fully meets all criteria.
He posits the following evaluative questions:
Have studies yielded empirically founded, universal truths?
Do generalizations apply to all regions or only specific locales?
What is the level of methodological and epistemological rigor in the field?
Do the theories actually possess substantial explanatory power?

II. Empirical Generalizations and Their Limits

===== PAGE 9 =====
After fifteen years of research, what has
the field actually found?

===== PAGE 10 =====
Empirical Generalization
“Groups of variables so related to each other as to explain successfully some
outcome but not justified in terms of more comprehensive intellectual
structure.”
1.  It’s a group of variables
2. Their link successfully explains an outcome. It works empirically
(observation/experience)
3. But it’s not part of a larger theory. It’s floating.

===== PAGE 11 =====
The Evidence Pile
13 verified findings, grouped: Global, Socialist blocs, Western Europe/EEC,
Late-developing (Africa/Latin America), External world. 
Highlights:
Common markets = strongest driver for Regional Integration
Perception of fairness determines whether interdependence feels
good/bad
In Africa, Regional Integration is mostly symbolic
The effect of outside (exogenous) powers is unpredictable
Overall, Haas (1970) calls it a "mixed bag", because these reside in different
levels of abstraction, unranked and unconnected.

===== PAGE 12 =====
Gaps and Problems
The truths in the Empirical Generalizations are multilayered in meaning and
implication. 
“One of the major problems implicit in this list of findings is the dependence of
most propositions on a number of explicit and implicit assumptions made prior
to the initiation of research. There is already a theoretical basis to the mere
listing of relevant variables. The findings are only as strong as the assumptions,
and the assumptions cannot be made evident without looking at the theories
which inspired them. This is true of the measures and indicators embedded in
various assumptions.”
Indefinite nature of the end state. Where is regional integration supposed to
end?

===== PAGE 13 =====
4 Major Gaps in Factual and Conceptual Knowledge
1. Do variables explaining the initiation of a union also explain its maintenance, as
we seem to have assumed?
2. Must we asssume--as we have--that decision-making styles at the national
level must be congruent with those at the regional level?
3. Are processes of integration in the underdeveloped world so different from
those in the West that we need two different theories or models? This raises
the important issue of the relative weight to be given to the variables in each
geographical setting.
4. How do actors learn? Do Perceptions of benefits from changing transactions
affect the definition of interests? Is there some other process of socialization at
work?

III. Three Pretheories of Regional Integration

===== PAGE 16 =====
Overview
Three conventions (Federalism, Communications, and Neo-functionalism) are
not yet fully validated theories.
They serve as "pretheories" that define the variables and research strategies
to study integration. 
Each approach postulates different causal links between variables
They also rely on distinct methodologies (aggregate data survey, case studies)

===== PAGE 17 =====
The Federalist Approach
Assumers that political purposes and institutions are transferable from the
national to the regional level.
It is preoccupied with institution-building, constitutional design, methods of
representation, and the division of powers.
It is divided between
Ideological activists (focused on realizing federation)
theorist-observers (focused on tracing federalizing processes)
The pure activist approach has been discredited in Europe and Africa while
theorists have moved closer to observing "processes" similar to neo-
functionalism.

===== PAGE 18 =====
The Communications Approach
It applies cybernetic theory  to human groupes, assuming that social
processes follow the same laws of transaction and response. 
It uses the volume of transactions (trade, mail, tourist flows) as the primary
measure of community formation. 
It seeks to explain the emergence of a "security community"
It is better at retroactive explanation than prediction and often ignores the
actual political content of messages.
It measures correlation, not causation. 
Transaction volume → trust → Security Community

===== PAGE 19 =====
T he Neo-Functional Approach
Borrows behavior patterns from pluralistic national states to explain regional-
level interactions.
It stressess self-interest and incremental decision-making.
Spillover - Work in one problem leads to unintended consequences and
demands for further integration in new sectors.
It leads to the creation of a political community.
Works when applied to Europe, mostly predicts failures elsewhere.

IV. Toward a Dependent Variable

===== PAGE 23 =====
Dependent Variable
The outcome that a theory is trying to explain or predict
How does a successful regional integration look like?

===== PAGE 24 =====
No Agreed Dependent Variable
The field's findings sit at inconsistent levels of abstraction. They are imperfectly
"integrated" knowledge
They are nonadditive. 
The deeper cause: no agreement on what the dependent variable even is.
Without a shared, observable dependent variable, findings from different
research traditions can never actually be combined. They were never
measuring the same thing

===== PAGE 25 =====
3 Rival Finish Lines
Federalism's endpoint
Federal union (a written constitution, divided powers, like the US or
Switzerland)
Communications theory's endpoint
Security community (amalgamated or pluralistic; a group that has stopped
expecting war with each other)
Neo-functionalism's endpoint: 
Political community / political union (narrower, assumes central institutions
and progressive centralization)

===== PAGE 26 =====
None of them is a true dependent variable. They
cannot be observed or measured to occurr in
reality

===== PAGE 27 =====
First alternative attempt: Scales as Dependent Variables
Instead of one single ideal-type endpoint, use quantitative multidimensional
scales
The end state becomes a "point" on a scale, or a convergence of several
scales measuring different dimensions
Problem:
There’s disagreement about which variables belong in the mix
It still assumes progress is always toward "higher" scores, foreclosing
other kinds of outcomes
It is useful as a methodological aid, but not itself an answer to the
dependent-variable problem

===== PAGE 28 =====
Second alternative attempt: Lindberg & Scheingold's
three outcomes
Leon Lindberg and Stuart Scheingold propose three possible outcomes for
any given integrative task:
Fulfillment — the task is achieved
Retraction — disintegration, the task backslides
Extension — the task spreads into areas not originally anticipated
Applied originally to the EEC/Euratom specifically, Haas suggests it could
extend to the wider collectivity of organizations around a bloc
Still needs a master concept to sum results across many issue areas — this is
the bridge to Haas's own fix

a. Use the 3 tools (fulfillment, extension, retraction)

===== PAGE 36 =====
O pen Questions Left Behind
1. Do forces that start integration also sustain it?
2. Must national and regional politics move in sync? (Haas: no — EEC kept
integrating economically while national politics got more nationalist)
3. Separate model for developing world? (Haas: same variables, different
weights)
4. How does "learning" actually work? (Haas's admitted biggest black box)
