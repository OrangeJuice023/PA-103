# Reading 15 — Gehring, Thomas (1996)

**Citation (WEB, accessed 2026-10-27):**
Gehring, Thomas. 1996. "Integrating Integration Theory: Neo-functionalism and International Regimes." *Global Society* 10(3): 225-253. DOI: 10.1080/13600829608443111.

**Status:** WEB — outside research, not from class slides. Do not present as professor content.

**1. What is "integration"?**
Gehring treats integration as institutionalized international governance: the development of an international governing institution (like the EC) within the horizontally structured international system. Integration is both an institution established by member states and a polity with intra-institutional decision processes.

**2. What drives it?**
Gehring bridges the divide between intergovernmentalism (static, state-centered) and neo-functionalism (dynamic, emphasizing non-state actors). He argues both are needed: states establish institutions, but institutions then enable non-state actor participation and hierarchical governance. The key is an institutional perspective that accounts for both state agency and institutional dynamics over time.

**3. What are the actors?**
Member states (who establish institutions), non-state and sub-state actors (who participate in intra-institutional decision-making), and the institution itself (which shapes actor decisions). States constitute a special class of actors.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Gehring develops a concept of "institutionalized international governance" that can be applied to both the EC and other international regimes. He keeps open the standard for measuring integration (substantive expansion, transnational dimension, or systemic strength).

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on both neo-functionalism (Haas, Schmitter) and regime theory (international regimes literature). Argues against the false dichotomy between intergovernmentalism and neo-functionalism. Argues against treating the EC as sui generis (needing its own theory) and against the claim that neo-functionalism only applies to the EC. Integrates both approaches through an institutional perspective.

**Cluster:** Institutions and governance.
