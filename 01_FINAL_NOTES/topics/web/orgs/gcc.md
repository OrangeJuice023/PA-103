## 1. What it is
A regional intergovernmental political and economic union of six Arab states of the Persian Gulf; headquartered in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia; the most institutionally developed Gulf sub-regional bloc.

## 2. Founding
- **Date:** 25 May 1981.
- **Place:** Riyadh, Saudi Arabia.
- **Founding members (6):** Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, United Arab Emirates. (Membership unchanged since founding.)

## 3. Members
6 members: Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, UAE. (Yemen has been in accession talks; no new members admitted.)

## 4. Purpose
Coordination and integration in economic, financial, and monetary affairs; customs union (2003) and common market (2008) aspirations; common defense (Peninsula Shield Force); foreign policy coordination; proposed (not realized) Gulf monetary union/currency.

## 5. Relevance
A security-plus-economic bloc shaped by shared monarchical regimes and oil wealth; rifts (e.g., 2017–2021 Qatar blockade) show integration limits; a key actor in Middle East security and energy markets.

## 6. Theory lens
**Security plus economic cooperation:** combines sub-regional defense (Peninsula Shield) with economic integration (customs union/common market); driven by regime security and shared identity as much as by market spillover — a security-led regionalism with an economic layer.
