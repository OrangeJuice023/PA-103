# Reading 14 — Stone Sweet, Alec, and Wayne Sandholtz (1997)

**Citation (WEB, accessed 2026-10-27):**
Stone Sweet, Alec, and Wayne Sandholtz. 1997. "European Integration and Supranational Governance." *Journal of European Public Policy* 4(3): 297-317. DOI: 10.1080/13501769780000011.

**Status:** WEB — outside research, not from class slides. Do not present as professor content.

**1. What is "integration"?**
A process in which national governance gives way to supranational governance: the competence of the EC to make binding rules in a given policy sector. Integration is provoked and sustained by causal connections between three factors: transnational exchange, supranational organization, and EC rule-making.

**2. What drives it?**
Two mechanisms: (1) cross-border transactions and communications generate social demand for EC rules and regulation, which supranational organizations supply; (2) once EC rules are in place, institutionalization ensues and provokes further integration. Community competences are unevenly constructed across sectors and over time, as a function of demand intensity. The theory is not compatible with intergovernmental theorizing.

**3. What are the actors?**
Transnational actors (generating demand), supranational organizations (supplying rules), member states (intergovernmental bargaining), and the EC as a whole. Non-state and sub-national actors play important roles.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Through testable propositions about how competences are constructed across sectors and over time. Intensive research has demonstrated the resilience of these propositions. The theory predicts uneven integration (faster in some policy domains than others).

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on neo-functionalism (Haas, Schmitter) and the "Laguna Beach Project" (Caporaso 1998). Argues against intergovernmentalism (Moravcsik): integration is not just state bargaining but driven by transnational demand and supranational supply. Also engages with the debate over whether the EU is sui generis or comparable to other organizations.

**Cluster:** Institutions and governance.
