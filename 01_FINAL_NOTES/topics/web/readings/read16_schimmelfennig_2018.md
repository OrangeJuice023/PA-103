# Reading 16 — Schimmelfennig, Frank (2018)

**Citation (WEB, accessed 2026-10-27):**
Schimmelfennig, Frank. 2018. "European Integration (Theory) in Times of Crisis: A Comparison of the Euro and Schengen Crises." *Journal of European Public Policy* 25(7): 969-989. DOI: 10.1080/13501763.2017.1421252.

**Status:** WEB — outside research, not from class slides. Do not present as professor content.

**1. What is "integration"?**
Integration is a process whose level, scope, or membership can increase or decrease. Schimmelfennig examines how crises affect integration: they may trigger disintegration or further integration depending on conditions.

**2. What drives it?**
Schimmelfennig puts forward a neofunctionalist explanation: variation in transnational interdependence and supranational capacity across policy areas explains different crisis outcomes. In the Euro crisis, strong financial market interdependence and a powerful ECB (supranational capacity) led to deeper integration. In the Schengen crisis, weak transnational interdependence and low supranational capacity (Frontex/EASO) meant no substantial integration progress.

**3. What are the actors?**
Governments, transnational actors (financial markets, migrants), supranational organizations (ECB, Frontex), and member state societies (politicization).

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Through comparative case analysis of the Euro and Schengen crises: structurally similar causes (exogenous shocks, functional shortcomings, distributional conflict, politicization) but different outcomes. Schimmelfennig tests neofunctionalism against postfunctionalism and intergovernmentalism, arguing that institutional path-dependence (earlier integration decisions) explains the variation better.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on neo-functionalism (Haas, Schmitter, Stone Sweet & Sandholtz). Argues against postfunctionalism (which attributes variation to politicization) and intergovernmentalism (which attributes outcomes to national interests and bargaining power). Argues that institutional and material path-dependencies override domestic politicization and intergovernmental conflict.

**Cluster:** Crisis and new regionalism.
