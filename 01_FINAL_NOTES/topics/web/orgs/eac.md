## 1. What it is
A regional intergovernmental organization of East African states aiming at economic, political, and social integration; HQ in Arusha, Tanzania; a revival of the earlier EAC (1967–1977) that collapsed.

## 2. Founding
- **Original EAC:** 1967 (Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda), collapsed 1977.
- **Revival:** Treaty for the Establishment of the EAC signed November 1999, in force July 2000; officially re-launched 2001; HQ Arusha, Tanzania.
- **Organs:** Summit of Heads of State; Council of Ministers; Co-ordinating Committee; Sectoral Committees; East African Court of Justice; East African Legislative Assembly; Secretariat.

## 3. Members
7 partner states: Burundi, DR Congo (joined 2022), Kenya, Rwanda, South Sudan, Tanzania, Uganda. (Original three: Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda.)

## 4. Purpose
Widen and deepen economic, political, social, and cultural integration; establish a customs union (2005), common market (2010), and move toward a monetary union and political federation; infrastructure and free movement of persons/labor/capital/services/goods.

## 5. Relevance
One of Africa's more ambitious integration projects (monetary union/political federation goals); shows both the potential and fragility of African integration (original collapse 1977 over political/economic tensions; revived 2000); relevant to Gibb's critique of African integration's state-led, uneven character.

## 6. Theory lens
**Economic integration (Balassa stages):** customs union → common market → monetary union → (proposed) political federation — a Balassa-style stage sequence in an African setting; **functionalism** in sectoral cooperation (infrastructure, transport, health) alongside political ambition.
