## 1. What it is
An international forum of 19 countries plus the European Union (and, since 2023, the African Union) that brings together the world's major advanced and emerging economies to coordinate on global economic policy; not a regional organization but a global-governance forum.

## 2. Founding
- **Date:** 1999 (finance ministers and central bank governors' forum), in response to the late-1990s Asian financial crisis.
- **Leaders' level:** first G20 Leaders' Summit held 2008 (Washington, D.C.) in response to the global financial crisis; since then the premier forum for international economic cooperation (per the 2009 Pittsburgh summit).
- Rotating annual presidency; no permanent secretariat (supported by a troika and host-country secretariat).

## 3. Members
19 countries + EU + African Union (2023): Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Canada, China, France, Germany, India, Indonesia, Italy, Japan, Mexico, Russia, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, South Korea, Turkey, UK, US, plus EU and AU.

## 4. Purpose
Coordinate global macroeconomic policy, financial regulation, trade, development, and (increasingly) climate, health, energy, and food security among systemically important economies.

## 5. Relevance
The principal steering group for global economic governance; reached peak salience 2008–2010 (fiscal stimulus, financial reform); now seen by some as constrained by geopolitical divisions (US–China, Russia–Ukraine) and the rise of minilateral alternatives.

## 6. Theory lens
**Global governance, not regional:** not regional integration at all — membership is global and issue-focused; useful as a contrast case in Part B to distinguish regional organizations from global forums (as flagged in the course table).
