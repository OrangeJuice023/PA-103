**1. What is "integration"?**
A process that aims to help member states strengthen their international position and protect themselves from undesirable external influence. Butorina and Borko argue that economic gain is not an immanent property of regional integration: it does not occur at all stages and is neither its primary goal nor its driving force.

**2. What drives it?**
Geopolitical goals and responses to the changing global order, not economic benefits. The original motives of EEC founders were geopolitical (post-WWII situation). Economic benefits (trade creation, single market) are important but not decisive. Multilateral integration associations with complex structures pursue strategic objectives that cannot be analyzed in accounting terms.

**3. What are the actors?**
Member states of regional associations, especially multilateral ones with complex structures (like the EU). Bilateral FTAs differ from multilateral integration.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Through cost-benefit analysis (trade creation/diversion, single market effects, OCA theory). Butorina and Borko argue that using net-benefit assessments as the only measure of integration effectiveness is unreasonable, especially for associations outside Europe.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on customs union theory (Viner) and OCA theory. Argues against the traditional view that economic benefits are the main driving force of regional integration. Challenges the consensus that integration should always bring net gain. Argues that geopolitical motives, not welfare effects, drive integration.

**Cluster:** Critique and measurement.
