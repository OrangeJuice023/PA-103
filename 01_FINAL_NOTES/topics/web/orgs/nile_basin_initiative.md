## 1. What it is
The Nile Basin Initiative (NBI) is a regional intergovernmental partnership of the Nile riparian states, launched on **22 February 1999** in Dar es Salaam, Tanzania. It was the first all-inclusive, basin-wide institutional arrangement for cooperation among the Nile riparians, created as a transitional mechanism pending a permanent legal framework. It succeeded TECCONILE (Technical Cooperation Committee for the Promotion of the Development and Environmental Protection of the Nile Basin, est. 1992). The name "Nile Basin Initiative" was endorsed by the Nile Council of Ministers (Nile-COM) in May 1999 in Addis Ababa.

## 2. Founding
- **Date:** 22 February 1999 (launch); May 1999 (name endorsed, Addis Ababa).
- **Place:** Dar es Salaam, Tanzania (launch).
- **Predecessor:** TECCONILE / Nile-COM (est. 1992); Nile-TAC first met 1998.
- **Legal status:** transitional institutional arrangement; the Nile Basin Initiative Act (2002) is a Ugandan Act conferring legal status in Uganda; NBI Headquarters Agreement (2002) with Uganda.
- **Drivers:** prior riparian cooperation on hydro-meteorological studies (1960s onward), post-Cold War support for regional integration to strengthen peace/security and foundations for development; driven by the Nile countries themselves.
- **External support:** World Bank as lead development partner and administrator of the multi-donor Nile Basin Trust Fund.

## 3. Members
**10 Member States:** Burundi, Democratic Republic of Congo, Egypt, Ethiopia, Kenya, Rwanda, South Sudan, Sudan, Tanzania, Uganda.
- **Eritrea:** participates as an observer (basin spans 11 countries including Eritrea).
- **Governing body:** Nile Council of Ministers of Water Affairs (Nile-COM) — highest governing body, rotational one-year chairmanship.
- **Technical body:** Nile Technical Advisory Committee (Nile-TAC) — senior civil servants, one representative + alternate per riparian.
- **Secretariat:** Nile-SEC in Entebbe, Uganda (executive and technical arm), jointly owned by all 10 member states.

## 4. Purpose
To manage and develop the shared Nile Basin water resources cooperatively; share substantial socio-economic benefits; and promote regional peace and security.
- **Shared Vision Objective:** "to achieve sustainable socio-economic development through equitable utilization of, and benefit from, the common Nile Basin water resources."
- **Three core functions:** (1) Facilitating Basin Cooperation — the only all-inclusive, neutral platform for riparians to discuss joint use of the Nile with trust and confidence; (2) Water Resources Management — technical capacity, shared knowledge bases, early warning systems; (3) Water Resources Development — preparing climate-resilient cooperative investments (energy, water, food).
- **Structure (subsidiarity):** Nile-SEC (Entebbe) plus two subsidiary action programs — ENTRO (Eastern Nile, Addis Ababa, Ethiopia) and NELSAP-CU (Nile Equatorial Lakes, Kigali, Rwanda).
- **Strategic Action Program (2003):** Shared Vision Program (8 projects) + Eastern Nile and Nile Equatorial Lakes subsidiary action programs.

## 5. Relevance / current developments
- **Cooperative Framework Agreement (CFA):** negotiated over more than a decade, opened for signature May 2010 (Entebbe); Egypt and Sudan froze participation in 2010 and declined to sign; the DRC abstained. Entered into force **13 October 2024** after ratification/accession by six states (Burundi, Ethiopia, Rwanda, Tanzania, Uganda; South Sudan acceded). The CFA triggers the Nile River Basin Commission (NRBC) to succeed the NBI (only six of the ten members had transitioned as of the 2025 Corporate Report). Article 14 (water security) was reserved/annexed for later resolution.
- **GERD context:** The Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam keeps Egypt–Sudan–Ethiopia hydro-politics live; NBI remains the main forum for riparian consultation despite the freeze/tensions.
- **Milestones:** Nile Day (22 February) launched 2007; first Nile Basin Heads of States Summit (Entebbe) 2017; NBI 10-Year Strategy launched 2017; CFA entry into force 2024.
- **Regional significance:** illustrates that functional cooperation on a shared resource can persist across decades of political tension, but also that distributional conflict (water allocation) can stall legal settlement.

## 6. Theory lens
**Functionalism (Mitrany):** cooperation on a concrete technical task (shared water management) builds trust, confidence, and habits of cooperation that can spill over into broader political integration. NBI is the textbook "technical task → regional cooperation" case.

**Secondary connections:**
- **Comparative regionalism beyond Europe** (Börzel & Risse, reading 26): a non-European, Africa-based intergovernmental organization; shows functional demand (security interdependence over a shared resource) can drive institution-building even where market interdependence is weak.
- **Gibb on Africa** (reading 27): NBI's elitist/state-led character and slow legal settlement (CFA) echo Gibb's critique of market-led integration in Africa and the sovereignty constraints of neo-patrimonial states.
- **Collective security** (readings 28–29): water as a potential source of conflict; NBI's dispute-resolution mechanism is an attempt at cooperative, non-zero-sum security over a vital resource.
