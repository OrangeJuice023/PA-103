## 1. What it is
A regional organization of Arab states in North Africa and the Middle East (West Asia), founded to promote Arab unity and coordinate political, economic, cultural, and social affairs; headquartered in Cairo, Egypt; operates by consensus with no enforcement power over members.

## 2. Founding
- **Date:** 22 March 1945 (Arab League Pact / Charter signed).
- **Place:** Cairo, Egypt.
- **Founding members (7):** Egypt, Iraq, Jordan (Transjordan), Lebanon, Saudi Arabia, Syria, Yemen. (Later joined by Libya 1953, Sudan/Tunisia/Morocco 1956, Kuwait/Algeria/etc. → 22 members today.)

## 3. Members
22 member states across North Africa and West Asia (the Arab world), including Palestine (represented by the PLO/PA). Suspensions/re-admissions have occurred (e.g., Egypt 1979–1989 after Camp David; Syria 2011–2023).

## 4. Purpose
Strengthen ties among Arab states, coordinate policies, safeguard independence and sovereignty, promote collective Arab interests, and serve as a forum for resolving intra-Arab disputes; cooperation on economic, cultural, and social affairs.

## 5. Relevance
The principal pan-Arab forum; often criticized for disunity and inaction on major crises (e.g., Iran–Iraq war, Gulf War, Syrian civil war, Gaza/Israel conflict), reflecting the tension between Arab identity/consensus and divergent state interests.

## 6. Theory lens
**Identity and community (Etzioni, Deutsch — readings 3/11):** integration based on shared language, culture, history, and identity rather than market spillover; a community-seeking organization where consensus and sovereignty-preservation limit supranational authority.
