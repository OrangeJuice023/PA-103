# Reading 12 — Puchala, Donald J. (1968)

**Citation (WEB, accessed 2026-10-27):**
Puchala, Donald J. 1968. "The Pattern of Contemporary Regional Integration." *International Studies Quarterly* 12(1): 38-64. DOI: 10.2307/3013557.

**Status:** WEB — outside research, not from class slides. Do not present as professor content. (Note: North Star flags year discrepancy — ISQ 12(1) is dated 1968; the course list says March 1969. The journal record confirms 1968.)

**1. What is "integration"?**
Puchala examines the pattern of contemporary regional integration as a multidimensional phenomenon. He is known for the "blind men and the elephant" metaphor: integration theory is like blind men touching different parts of an elephant — each captures one aspect but none sees the whole. Integration is too complex for any single theory or approach.

**2. What drives it?**
International transactions (flows of economic exchange, communication) build community and integration (transactionalist logic). But Puchala argues that transaction analysis fits some aspects of integration but not others — integration is multidimensional and no single approach suffices.

**3. What are the actors?**
States, nations, and transnational actors carrying on international transactions.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Through transaction analysis — operationalizing transaction flows as indicators of integration. Puchala's 1970 article "International Transactions and Regional Integration" (IO 24(4)) further develops this. He occupies a middle position: early exponents overestimated transaction analysis, early critics over-dismissed it.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on Deutsch et al. (*Political Community and the North Atlantic Area*) and Etzioni (transactional/community tradition). Argues against both the over-enthusiastic early transaction school and its dismissive critics. Integration is multidimensional, so no single approach (transactional, neo-functional, or federalist) suffices.

**Cluster:** Transactionalism / Critique and measurement.
