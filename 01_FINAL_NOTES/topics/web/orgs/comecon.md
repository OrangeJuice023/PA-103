## 1. What it is
The Soviet-bloc economic organization (1949–1991), formally the Council for Mutual Economic Assistance, coordinating economic development and trade among communist states of Central/Eastern Europe and allied countries; the Eastern bloc's counterpart to the OEEC/EEC.

## 2. Founding
- **Date:** January 1949 (established).
- **Place:** Moscow.
- **Founding members:** USSR, Bulgaria, Czechoslovakia, Hungary, Poland, Romania; Albania joined 1949 (withdrew 1960s), East Germany (GDR) 1950, Mongolia 1962, Cuba 1972, Vietnam 1978.
- **Dissolved:** 1991 (after the collapse of communist regimes in Eastern Europe).

## 3. Members
~10 members at peak (USSR + East European satellites + Cuba, Mongolia, Vietnam). The USSR was the dominant/hegemon member.

## 4. Purpose
Promote economic integration and coordinated development among socialist countries; specialize production, coordinate five-year plans, facilitate trade among members, reduce dependence on Western markets; provide aid/technical assistance to members.

## 5. Relevance
A case of integration organized and dominated by a hegemon (the USSR) rather than voluntary spillover; dissolved with the end of the Cold War and the transition of members to market economies; many members later joined the EU/NATO.

## 6. Theory lens
**Economic integration (Balassa)** and **integration by a hegemon:** staged economic coordination but imposed top-down rather than market-driven; contrasts with the voluntary neo-functionalist European model. Illustrates that integration can be coerced and can dissolve when the sponsoring power weakens.
