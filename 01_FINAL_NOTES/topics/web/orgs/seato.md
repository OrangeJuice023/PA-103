## 1. What it is
A Cold War collective-defense pact in Southeast Asia/the Pacific, created under the 1954 Manila Pact; often described as the "NATO of Southeast Asia," though it was much weaker (no integrated command, no automatic collective-response obligation) and was dissolved in 1977.

## 2. Founding
- **Date:** 8 September 1954 (Manila Pact signed).
- **Place:** Manila, Philippines.
- **Founding members (8):** Australia, France, New Zealand, Pakistan, the Philippines, Thailand, the United Kingdom, the United States. (Thailand and the Philippines were the only Southeast Asian members.)

## 3. Members
8 members: Australia, France, New Zealand, Pakistan, Philippines, Thailand, UK, US. Plus "associated" South Vietnam, Laos, Cambodia (via protocol, not full members).

## 4. Purpose
To contain communist expansion in Southeast Asia and the Southwest Pacific (the "domino theory" context); collective defense against aggression in the treaty area; consultation in case of threat to peace.

## 5. Relevance
Dissolved 30 June 1977 after failing to prevent communist victories in Vietnam/Laos/Cambodia and lacking cohesion (members pursued their own policies, e.g., France's withdrawal from NATO's military command; Pakistan's shift after 1971); illustrates limits of Cold War, extra-regional-led security pacts in Southeast Asia; relevant to Philippine history (host of the signing, a US ally).

## 6. Theory lens
**Collective security:** a Cold War regional collective-defense arrangement; like NATO a security-based pact, but its collapse shows collective-security/defense pacts depend on shared threat perceptions and member commitment — the limits Clark and Tarzi (readings 28–29) identify.
