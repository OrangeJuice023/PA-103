## 1. What it is
An intergovernmental organization of petroleum-producing countries that coordinates oil-production and export policy to stabilize oil markets and secure fair and stable prices for producers; headquartered in Vienna, Austria.

## 2. Founding
- **Date:** 10–14 September 1960.
- **Place:** Baghdad, Iraq.
- **Founding members (5):** Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, Venezuela.
- Expanded to include Qatar (1961), Indonesia (1962), Libya (1962), UAE (1967), Algeria (1969), Nigeria (1971), Ecuador (1973), Gabon (1975), Angola (2007), Equatorial Guinea (2017), Congo (2018), with several suspensions/exits/re-joins (e.g., Qatar left 2019, Ecuador suspended/exited, Indonesia suspended/exited).

## 3. Members
12 members (as of mid-2020s): Algeria, Congo, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Libya, Nigeria, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Venezuela. (OPEC+ is OPEC plus 10 non-OPEC producers led by Russia — a broader coordination arrangement, not OPEC membership.)

## 4. Purpose
Coordinate and unify petroleum policies among members; stabilize oil markets; secure an efficient, economic, and regular supply of petroleum to consumers; a fair and stable return for producers.

## 5. Relevance
A commodity/sectoral cartel that can move global oil prices (notably the 1973 oil embargo/shock); shows sectoral cooperation can yield great geopolitical leverage despite members' political differences; influence has waxed and waned with market conditions and OPEC+ coordination.

## 6. Theory lens
**Sectoral cooperation:** integration limited to one commodity (oil) rather than broad economic or political union; members pool only production policy, guarding sovereignty otherwise — a narrow functional/sectoral arrangement rather than classical integration.
