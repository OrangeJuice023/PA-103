# Reading 29 — Tarzi, Shah M. (1997)

**Citation (WEB, accessed 2026-10-27):**
Tarzi, Shah M. 1997. "The Dilemma of Collective Security: A Theoretical Critique." *International Relations* 13(6): 43-53. DOI: 10.1177/004711789701300604. (Published 1997-12-01.)
- https://doi.org/10.1177/004711789701300604
- https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/004711789701300604

**Status:** WEB — outside research, not from class slides. Do not present as professor content.

**1. What is "integration"?**
Collective security as a theoretical framework for security cooperation among states — a conceptual/institutional form of integration aimed at mutual security without reliance on unilateral power balancing.

**2. What drives it?**
Security interdependence and the collective-security dilemma: the aspiration to replace balance-of-power/power-politics logic with collective action, and the theoretical problems that arise when great-power interests diverge.

**3. What are the actors?**
States (especially great powers), the United Nations / Security Council, collective-security institutions.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Theoretical critique/conceptual analysis of the assumptions and logic of collective security rather than empirical measurement.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on the collective-security literature (Wolfers, Jervis, Betts, the League/UN tradition). Argues against the theoretical foundations/assumptions of collective security — the "dilemma" that collective security requires conditions (great-power congruence) it cannot itself create. Pairs with Clark's practical critique.

**Cluster:** Collective security.
