# Reading 27 — Gibb, Richard (2009)

**Citation (WEB, accessed 2026-10-27):**
Gibb, Richard. 2009. "Regional Integration and Africa's Development Trajectory: Meta-theories, Expectations and Reality." *Third World Quarterly* 30(4): 701-721. DOI: 10.1080/01436590902867136.
- https://doi.org/10.1080/01436590902867136

**Status:** WEB — outside research, not from class slides. Do not present as professor content.

**1. What is "integration"?**
A process understood through Western/Euro-centric, market-led conceptions of regionalism — which the author argues produce a biased understanding of African regionalism and have, in practice, failed.

**2. What drives it?**
The market-led / neo-classical development paradigm (modernity, development) imposed from outside Africa. In reality, African integration is elitist and state-led, within neo-patrimonial states; constrained by lack of complementarity, small/uncompetitive economies, imperfect markets, protectionism, unfavorable terms of trade.

**3. What are the actors?**
African states (neo-patrimonial), elites, state-led regional organizations; sovereignty is not readily shared under a Westphalian/neo-patrimonial view.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Critique of market-led integration metrics; argues African regionalism is shallow, as strong as its weakest member, producing a "spaghetti bowl" of contradictory agreements; states assent formally but do not subscribe to the neo-classical market model.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Argues against applying the European market-integration paradigm to Africa (Euro-centric, hegemonic); argues that sovereignty and neo-patrimonial politics limit real political commitment; favors a more nuanced, context-specific view of African regionalism.

**Cluster:** Crisis and new regionalism (Gibb on Africa).
