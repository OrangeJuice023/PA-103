# Reading 5 — Caporaso, James (1998)

**Citation (WEB, accessed 2026-10-27):**
Caporaso, James A. 1998. "Regional Integration Theory: Understanding Our Past and Anticipating Our Future." *Journal of European Public Policy* 5(1): 1-16. DOI: 10.1080/13501768880000011.

**Status:** WEB — outside research, not from class slides. Do not present as professor content.

**1. What is "integration"?**
A process of institutionalization of the European Community/EU. Caporaso treats integration as a long-term process of institution-building in which the EU has moved from a loose association toward deeper governance structures.

**2. What drives it?**
Institutional change and path dependence: historical decisions shape current frameworks. Caporaso traces three themes: (1) growing institutionalization of the EC over time; (2) changing relevance of different theories over time (shift from neo-functionalism to liberal intergovernmentalism, then to comparative politics approaches); (3) the current state of theoretical debate.

**3. What are the actors?**
Member states, supranational institutions (Commission, Court), and scholars/theorists. The field itself has evolved alongside the EU.

**4. How is it measured or tested?**
Through historical-institutional analysis and comparative politics approaches. Caporaso reflects on the field's evolution rather than proposing a single metric.

**5. Builds on / argues against?**
Builds on the "Laguna Beach Project" (co-organized by Stone Sweet and Sandholtz, published as *Supranational Governance*, 1997). Argues that theories of integration have changed in relevance over time: neo-functionalism dominated early, liberal intergovernmentalism (Moravcsik) rose later, and comparative politics approaches became more important as the EU matured. Contrasts with Haas's early neo-functionalism by emphasizing institutionalization and path dependence.

**Cluster:** Institutions and governance.
